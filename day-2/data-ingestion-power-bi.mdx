## Part 1: Load and clean with Power Query (15 min)

Power Query is the **ETL** tool inside Power BI: **E**xtract, **T**ransform, **L**oad.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Extract: load the CSV">
    **Home** → **Get data** → **Text/CSV** → select `dataset.csv` → **Transform Data** (not Load).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Transform: remove the junk index column">
    If there is a first column called `Column1` or `Unnamed: 0`, right-click it → **Remove**. It is only a row number.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Transform: fix data types">
    Click the small icon in each column header. Set `popularity` to **Whole Number**; `danceability`, `energy`, `valence`, `acousticness`, `tempo` and similar to **Decimal Number**; `explicit` to **True/False**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Transform: remove blank rows">
    Select `track_name` → **Home** → **Remove Rows** → **Remove Blank Rows**. A handful of tracks have no name.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Transform: remove duplicates">
    Select `track_id` → right-click → **Remove Duplicates**. Why? The same track appears once per genre it belongs to.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the target column">
    **Add Column** → **Custom Column**, name it `viral_label`, formula:

    ```text
    if [popularity] >= 60 then "Viral" else "Not viral"
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Load">
    **Home** → **Close & Apply**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

{/* ILLUSTRATION I-16: Before/after Power Query screenshot pair with a draggable divider. Annotate each step number on the "applied steps" panel. */}

<Note>
  The threshold `60` is a starting choice. On the cleaned data (about 73,600 unique tracks) it labels roughly 12–13% of tracks as viral, which leaves enough viral examples for a model to learn from. If your share is very different, adjust the number, and use the **same number** in Power BI and Colab.
</Note>

## Part 2: Build the dashboard (25 min)

We are building one dark, single-page dashboard with three panels, in the style of a streaming app.

<DashboardWireframe />

| Panel | What it answers |
| --- | --- |
| **Overview** | How big is the catalogue, and how much of it goes viral? |
| **Genres** | Which genres are most popular? |
| **Sound** | What do viral tracks sound like compared with the rest? |

<Tip>
  **Facilitators:** the styling steps are the slowest part. Hand students a starter `.pbix` with the theme, the dark page and the three empty panels already set up, so they spend the 25 minutes building visuals rather than formatting.
</Tip>

### Step 0: Apply the theme (2 min)

A theme restyles every visual at once, so nothing has a white background on a dark page.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Save the theme file">
    Open the **Theme file** box at the end of this page, copy the text into Notepad, and save it as `spotify-dark.json`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Import it">
    **View** → **Themes** → **Browse for themes** → select `spotify-dark.json`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the canvas size">
    Click an empty area of the page, then **Format page** → **Page size** → **16:9** (1280 × 720).
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Step 1: Group the genres into families (3 min)

There are 114 genres, far too many for a slicer or a readable chart. We will group them into a handful of **families**.

Right-click your table in the **Data** pane → **New column**, then paste:

```text
Genre Family =
SWITCH(
    TRUE(),
    'dataset'[track_genre] IN {"pop", "indie-pop", "power-pop", "synth-pop", "k-pop", "j-pop", "cantopop", "mandopop", "pop-film", "j-idol"}, "Pop",
    'dataset'[track_genre] IN {"rock", "alt-rock", "alternative", "hard-rock", "psych-rock", "punk-rock", "punk", "grunge", "emo", "goth", "industrial", "ska", "rockabilly", "rock-n-roll", "j-rock", "indie", "guitar", "metal", "heavy-metal", "black-metal", "death-metal", "metalcore", "grindcore", "hardcore"}, "Rock & Metal",
    'dataset'[track_genre] IN {"hip-hop", "r-n-b", "soul", "funk", "groove", "trip-hop", "gospel"}, "Hip-hop & R&B",
    'dataset'[track_genre] IN {"edm", "electro", "electronic", "dance", "club", "house", "deep-house", "chicago-house", "progressive-house", "techno", "minimal-techno", "detroit-techno", "trance", "hardstyle", "dubstep", "drum-and-bass", "breakbeat", "idm", "dub", "garage", "disco", "j-dance", "party"}, "Electronic & Dance",
    'dataset'[track_genre] IN {"latin", "latino", "reggaeton", "salsa", "samba", "pagode", "sertanejo", "forro", "mpb", "brazil", "tango", "spanish", "reggae", "dancehall", "afrobeat", "world-music", "indian", "french", "german", "swedish", "turkish", "iranian", "malay"}, "Latin & World",
    'dataset'[track_genre] IN {"acoustic", "ambient", "chill", "sleep", "study", "new-age", "piano", "sad", "singer-songwriter", "songwriter", "folk", "country", "bluegrass", "honky-tonk"}, "Chill & Acoustic",
    'dataset'[track_genre] IN {"jazz", "blues", "classical", "opera", "show-tunes"}, "Jazz & Classical",
    "Other"
)
```

<Note>
  If your table isn't called `dataset`, replace `'dataset'` with its name. Any genre not listed falls into **Other**.
</Note>

**Check it:** add a quick **Table** visual with `Genre Family` and **Count of** `track_id`. You should see eight families, with **Other** as the smallest group. Delete the table afterwards.

### Step 2: Lay out the page (3 min)

Add three **Rounded rectangle** shapes (**Insert** → **Shapes**) as panel backgrounds, then place everything using **Format** → **General** → **Properties** → **Size and position**. Using the same numbers keeps the page tidy and aligned.

| Item | X | Y | Width | Height |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Title text box | 20 | 12 | 520 | 56 |
| Genre family slicer | 560 | 14 | 520 | 56 |
| Explicit dropdown | 1096 | 14 | 164 | 56 |
| Panel 1 (Overview) | 20 | 84 | 402 | 616 |
| Panel 2 (Genres) | 438 | 84 | 402 | 616 |
| Panel 3 (Sound) | 856 | 84 | 402 | 616 |

Give each panel shape the fill `#181818`, and add a small text label ("OVERVIEW", "GENRES", "SOUND") near the top of each.

### Step 3: Cards, the headline numbers (4 min)

Add three **Card** visuals in Panel 1, side by side (each about 120 wide and 90 tall, at Y 124):

- **Count of** `track_id`, renamed "Tracks"
- **Average of** `popularity` (not Sum), renamed "Avg popularity"
- A **Viral Share** measure. **Modeling** → **New measure**:

```text
Viral Share = DIVIDE(COUNTROWS(FILTER('dataset', 'dataset'[popularity] >= 60)), COUNTROWS('dataset'))
```

Format it as a percentage.

### Step 4: Donut, viral vs not viral (2 min)

**Donut chart** under the cards. **Legend:** `viral_label`. **Values:** **Count of** `track_id`. In **Format visual** → **Slices** → **Colors**, set Viral to green (`#1DB954`) and Not viral to grey (`#535353`). Turn on detail labels as **Percent of total**.

### Step 5: Which genres are most popular? (3 min)

In Panel 2, add a **Clustered bar chart**. **Y-axis:** `track_genre`. **X-axis:** **Average of** `popularity`. In the **Filters** pane set **Top N** to 10 by that value, then switch on **Data labels**.

### Step 6: Energy vs happiness by genre (4 min)

In Panel 3, add a **Scatter chart** with these fields:

| Well | Field |
| --- | --- |
| **X Axis** | Average of `energy` |
| **Y Axis** | Average of `valence` (the "happiness" of a track) |
| **Details** | `track_genre` |
| **Size** | Count of `track_id` |
| **Legend** | `Genre Family` |
| **Play Axis** | _leave empty_ |

Now each bubble is a **genre**, coloured by family, so you get a readable cloud of about 114 bubbles instead of 73,000 dots.

<Warning>
  **Only see one bubble, with a big word floating on the chart and a play slider underneath?** A field is in the **Play Axis** well instead of **Details**. Drag it out of Play Axis and into Details. The play axis shows one item at a time, which is why only one bubble appears.
</Warning>

### Step 7: What do viral tracks sound like? (3 min)

Under the scatter plot add a **Clustered column chart**. **X-axis:** `viral_label`. **Y-axis:** add four fields, each set to **Average**: `energy`, `danceability`, `valence`, `acousticness`. Now you can compare two groups across four audio features at a glance. This chart is the best evidence for your hypotheses in the next session.

### Step 8: Slicers (3 min)

<Steps>
  <Step title="Genre family slicer">
    Add a **Slicer** using `Genre Family`. In **Format visual** → **Slicer settings** → **Options** → **Style**, choose **Tile**. Eight buttons fit neatly along the top.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Explicit slicer">
    Add another **Slicer** using `explicit`, and set its style to **Dropdown**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Try it">
    Hold **Ctrl** and click two families. Every visual updates.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Check>
  Your dashboard should have three cards, a donut, a top-10 genre bar chart, a scatter plot of genre bubbles, a viral vs not-viral comparison, and two slicers that filter everything.
</Check>

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="A chart has a white background">
    The theme wasn't applied. Re-import it from **View** → **Themes**, or set the visual's **Effects** → **Background** to `#181818`.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The slicer is too long or won't fit">
    You're slicing by `track_genre` (114 values). Use `Genre Family` instead, with the **Tile** style.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Numbers look huge or meaningless">
    A field is set to **Sum**. Click its dropdown in the Fields well and choose **Average**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Bubbles all overlap in a corner">
    Set both scatter axes to range 0 to 1: **Format visual** → **X axis** or **Y axis** → **Range**.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
